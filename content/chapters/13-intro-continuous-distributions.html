CHAPTER 13 · LESSON NOTES

Introduction: Continuous Probability Distributions

Starting a new session, this time on continuous random variables and the normal distribution.

The last session covered discrete probability distributions: the binomial distribution and the uniform distribution, along with the idea of cumulative probability. This session builds on those ideas by moving from discrete random variables (like the number of red balls in the bag game, which could only be 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4) to continuous ones, where a value can fall anywhere along a range instead of on a fixed list of outcomes.

In this session

You will learn:

  • Cumulative probability in a little more depth, building on the introduction from the last session.
  • How the probability of a continuous random variable is expressed, and why it works differently from the discrete case.
  • The normal distribution, the most commonly used probability distribution for continuous random variables.

Prerequisites

This session assumes you already know:

  • What a random variable and a probability distribution are, covered in Chapter 4, Random Variables, and Chapter 5, Probability Distributions.
  • Cumulative probability, F(x) = P(X ≤ x), introduced in Chapter 11, Cumulative Probability Distributions.
